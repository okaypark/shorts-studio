# Product shorts direction

Use this reference when a product, package, store page, or sales intent is present.

## Product-first composition

- Reveal the real product or its use context in the first 0-2 seconds. A mystery hook may delay the name, but not the category or visual subject.
- In at least one hero shot, the visible product should occupy roughly 35-55% of the frame width. If the source image contains transparent padding, judge the visible package, not the image element's CSS box.
- Show the product meaningfully at least twice: an early reveal and a later use, proof, or CTA shot. Avoid keeping it small in the far-right UI zone.
- Use hands, pouring, opening, wearing, applying, packing, or another manufacturer-intended interaction when practical. A floating pack shot alone rarely explains why the viewer should care.
- Keep recognizable packaging faithful. Use the official supplied image for the package and generated imagery for environments, props, lighting, or backgrounds.

## 1080x1920 type scale

These are starting ranges, not templates. Verify at phone scale and adjust for font width and text length.

- Hook or hero phrase: 116-168 px, usually one or two lines.
- Scene headline: 92-132 px.
- Spoken phrase caption: 46-64 px, one or two lines, about 4-8 Korean eojeol per card.
- Reaction or keyword: 68-120 px.
- Label or evidence note: 24-34 px; use sparingly because smaller text is easy to lose.

Use heavy weight, high contrast, and a stable plate or outline when the background moves. Scale the box and line breaks before shrinking the font. Left alignment works when the product clearly owns the center or right; centered text works when the background supplies a clean central band; side switching is allowed when the subject moves.

## Consumer-first script

Build around one consumer-relevant idea:

`recognizable moment -> curiosity or tension -> product reveal -> one useful benefit or selection reason -> intended use -> clear next step`

- Do not open with the brand name or a list of ingredients unless recognition itself is the hook.
- Translate specifications and ingredients into why they matter in the viewer's routine. Keep only the ingredient or feature needed to support the main idea.
- Use visible action to carry information that narration does not need to repeat.
- The large headline compresses the idea; phrase captions carry speech; a reaction caption provides humor. Do not duplicate the same sentence in all three layers.
- For regulated products, use only verified labels or supplied official material. Humor cannot imply a stronger benefit than the spoken claim.

## Suggested 20-30 second rhythm

- 0-2s: visual interruption plus viewer situation; product category already legible.
- 2-6s: product reveal or hands-on action.
- 6-14s: one reason to care, demonstrated visually.
- 14-22s: intended audience or use moment; optional reaction beat.
- 22-30s: usage instruction or choice criterion, large product hero, concise CTA.

Do not stretch a weak idea to fill this timing. Let measured narration determine the exact cut points.
