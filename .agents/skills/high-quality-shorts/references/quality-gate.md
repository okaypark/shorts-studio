# Rendered quality gate

Complete this gate on the rendered output, not only the source code.

## First-glance test

- At 25% desktop zoom or a phone-sized preview, can a viewer identify the subject in under two seconds?
- Is the intended hero the largest or highest-contrast meaningful object?
- Does every frame have one obvious first, second, and third reading target?
- If the product is present, is the visible package large enough to recognize without pausing?
- Does the main reading path stay near the optical center, with a deliberate reason for any side-heavy composition?
- Does the first three seconds create both product recognition and curiosity?

## Frame inspection

Inspect the start, middle, and end of every scene plus the longest caption, largest animation, first frame, product reveal, humor beat, and final CTA. Check:

- crop, transparency, broken image text, watermarks, and package fidelity;
- headline/product/caption collisions and right-side platform controls;
- top 10%, bottom 25%, and right 10% UI risks for a common master;
- Korean word breaks, three-line captions, insufficient contrast, and text smaller than its role requires;
- product visible size rather than CSS container size;
- narration ending before transition and captions appearing with the spoken phrase;
- generated backgrounds matching the reserved text and subject slots.

## Quality failures that require revision

- The product appears only after the explanation has already started.
- A decorative background or headline attracts more attention than the product or person.
- Every scene repeats the same left-title/card layout.
- Full narration sentences sit in one fixed bottom caption band throughout.
- Product, face, or CTA falls under platform UI.
- A supplied official image was replaced with an invented package.
- The design is technically clean but indistinguishable from a presentation slide.
- Scene numbers, small labels, or repeated static headings consume attention without increasing product understanding, curiosity, or purchase desire.
- More than 2.5 seconds pass without a meaningful visual change during the opening and explanation.

After correction, render to a new filename and inspect the changed frames again. Do not claim the whole video was visually verified when only a sample was checked.
